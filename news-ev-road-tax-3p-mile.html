How a New 3p per Mile EV Road Tax Could Affect You

By MyEVReport Team · Updated December 2025

Talk of a new “3p per mile tax” on electric vehicles has raised plenty of questions for current and future EV drivers. The idea is simple: instead of paying traditional fuel duty at the pump, EV owners would pay a distance-based charge for the roads they use.

In this guide we’re not taking a political view. Instead, we’ll walk through what a 3p per mile charge could mean in practical terms – using real-world journey examples – so you can quickly see how it might affect your running costs.

Quick summary

  • A 3p per mile road charge would add £3 for every 100 miles you drive.
  • On a typical 8,000–10,000 mile year, that’s around £240–£300 annually.
  • EVs would likely still be cheaper to run than petrol and diesel in most cases, but the gap narrows.
  • The biggest impact will be on high-mileage drivers and long-distance commuters.

What is the proposed 3p per mile EV tax?

The idea of a per-mile charge is aimed at replacing some of the fuel duty and vehicle tax income that governments currently receive from petrol and diesel drivers. As more people switch to electric, those traditional revenues fall – so policymakers are exploring new ways of charging for road use.

Under a simple version of the scheme, an EV driver would pay: 3 pence for every mile driven, recorded either by mileage readings, connected car data, or smart charging infrastructure.

Real-world examples: what would you actually pay?

To make this concrete, here are a few example journeys and what a 3p per mile charge would add on top of your usual charging costs:

Journey Approx. miles (one way) 3p per mile cost
Newcastle to Glasgow 332 miles £9.96
Manchester to Brighton 519 miles £15.57
Edinburgh to Bristol 746 miles £22.38
Sheffield to Leeds 78 miles £2.34
Oxford to Cambridge 204 miles £6.12

For many drivers, the numbers look relatively small on a single trip. The real question is: what does it mean over a year?

What it might mean per year

Here’s how an annual 3p per mile charge could add up for different types of EV driver:

  • Low-mileage city driver (5,000 miles / year)
    5,000 × £0.03 = £150 per year
  • Typical private driver (8,000 miles / year)
    8,000 × £0.03 = £240 per year
  • High-mileage commuter (12,000 miles / year)
    12,000 × £0.03 = £360 per year
  • Frequent long-distance driver (20,000 miles / year)
    20,000 × £0.03 = £600 per year

That’s on top of your electricity costs, parking, insurance and any other local charges. EVs would often still be cheaper per mile than petrol or diesel — especially if you charge at home on an off-peak tariff — but the savings margin shrinks.

Who would feel the impact most?

A flat 3p per mile charge affects different drivers in different ways:

  • High-mileage drivers (sales reps, gig workers, frequent motorway users) see the biggest numbers.
  • City-based drivers who mostly do short trips may feel it less, but could still face congestion or clean air zone charges on top.
  • Drivers on tight budgets who switched to an EV for lower running costs will want to keep a close eye on how much they actually drive each year.

Why battery health still matters more than ever

Whatever happens with future road taxes, one thing doesn’t change: the health of your EV battery is still the single biggest factor in how far you can go on a charge, and how much your car is worth.

If a distance-based tax is introduced, an EV with a strong, healthy battery is even more valuable:

  • You get more real-world miles from each charge.
  • You can plan journeys more confidently, without unexpected rapid-charging stops.
  • Your EV is likely to be more attractive to future buyers, private or trade.
